When Mr. Collins had anything to say which he wished should be kept secret, he always wrote it down. He had no secrets from his wife, however; and all his letters, whether addressed to her or not, were submitted to her inspection before they were sealed. He had been married three days before he had the pleasure of receiving a letter from Mr. Bennet, which was as follows:—

"Dear Sir,

I have received your letter, and am very much obliged to you for the very flattering account you give of my daughter Elizabeth. I hope she will turn out as well as you expect. I shall take care to let her know your opinion of her. You may depend upon it that I shall not forget your kindness."

Mr. Collins read this letter to his wife, and they both agreed that Mr. Bennet was a very civil man. Mr. Collins then wrote a long letter in reply, and after it was finished, he read it aloud to his wife. It was as follows:—

"My dear Madam,

Your letter of the 1st instant gave me great satisfaction. The warmth of your welcome, and the kindness with which you received me at Hunsford, will never be forgotten by me. I hope you will be so good as to present my compliments to Lady Catherine and her daughter. I hope they are both well. I trust that my dear Charlotte is happy. She has so good a temper, and so good an understanding, that I cannot help thinking she will be a very happy wife. I hope she will be a credit to her family."

Mr. Collins folded up his letter, and directed it to his cousin. His wife then asked him whether he had anything more to say about the living. He said he had not. He had not been able to get any intelligence about it. He did not know whether Mr. Darcy had anything to do with it or not.

The next day Mr. Collins went to wait upon Lady Catherine. He was received in the drawing-room, where Lady Catherine and her daughter were sitting. Lady Catherine asked him many questions about his wife and her family. Mr. Collins answered them all with great deference. He told her that his wife was very well, and that he hoped she and her daughter were in good health. He also told her that he had received a letter from Mr. Bennet, which he read to her.

Lady Catherine was not pleased with the letter. She thought Mr. Bennet was a very impertinent man. She said she did not like his tone at all. Mr. Collins agreed with her, but he did not dare to say so very strongly.

After a long conversation, Mr. Collins took his leave. He was very glad to get away from Lady Catherine, for he was always afraid of her. He went back to his parsonage, where he found his wife waiting for him. She asked him what Lady Catherine had said about the living. He told her that Lady Catherine had not said anything about it. She was very much disappointed, but she said she would wait and see.

In the meantime, Mr. Bingley and his sisters had gone to London. They were to stay there for the winter. Mr. Bingley promised to come back in the spring. Jane was very sorry to hear that he was going away, but she tried not to show it. Elizabeth was also sorry, but she was glad that Mr. Darcy was going too. She did not want to see him again.

The winter passed away very slowly. Jane and Elizabeth wrote to each other every week. Jane told Elizabeth about her life in London, and Elizabeth told Jane about her life at Longbourn. They both talked about Mr. Bingley and Mr. Darcy, but they did not say very much. They were both afraid of saying too much.

In the spring, Mr. Bingley came back to Netherfield. He came alone, without his sisters. He came to see Jane. He stayed for a week, and then he went back to London. Jane was very disappointed, but she tried not to show it. Elizabeth was also disappointed, but she was glad that Mr. Darcy did not come back with Mr. Bingley. She did not want to see him again.

Mr. Collins came to visit his wife's family at Longbourn. He stayed for a week. He was very civil to everybody. He talked a great deal about Lady Catherine and her daughter. He also talked a great deal about his own living. He said that Lady Catherine had given him a very good living, and that he was very grateful to her. Elizabeth did not like Mr. Collins. She thought he was a very foolish man. But she was polite to him, because she did not want to hurt his feelings.

Mr. Collins asked Elizabeth to marry him. Elizabeth refused him. Mr. Collins was very much surprised, but he did not seem to be very much hurt. He said that he would ask her again some other time. Elizabeth said that she would never marry him. Mr. Collins then asked Charlotte Lucas to marry him, and she accepted him. Elizabeth was very surprised to hear this, but she was glad that Charlotte was happy.

Mr. Bingley came back to Netherfield in the autumn. He came with Mr. Darcy. They stayed for a few weeks. Mr. Bingley paid a great deal of attention to Jane. Mr. Darcy paid very little attention to Elizabeth. Elizabeth was very glad of this. She did not want to encourage him.

One day, Mr. Darcy asked Elizabeth to walk with him. She agreed. They walked together for a long time. Mr. Darcy told her that he loved her. Elizabeth was very surprised. She did not know what to say. She told him that she could not marry him. Mr. Darcy was very much surprised, but he did not seem to be very much hurt. He said that he would ask her again some other time. Elizabeth said that she would never marry him.

Mr. Darcy went away very soon after this. He went back to London. Elizabeth was very glad that he had gone. She did not want to see him again.
